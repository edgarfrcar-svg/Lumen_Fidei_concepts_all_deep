About Lumen Fidei

A cinematic digital encyclopedia of Christian theology spanning the Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, and Protestant traditions. The tone is that of a museum exhibit, a historical documentary, and a sacred archive — dramatic, reverent, and visually rich.

Editorial Posture

All biographical, doctrinal, and summary content on this site is written specifically for Lumen Fidei. It is not scraped or lightly reworded from other websites. Sources include primary texts (where public domain), classical denominational scholarship, and reliable modern historical work from Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant authors alike.

The overall theological posture is traditionally-leaning within each tradition: classical and confessional positions are presented as each tradition has historically taught them. Points of doctrinal disagreement between the three traditions are marked clearly and neutrally rather than flattened.

Images — Strict Public-Domain Rule

No AI-generated images appear anywhere on this site.

Every portrait, icon, and artwork is drawn from verifiable public-domain sources only:

  • Wikimedia Commons (files tagged PD-old, PD-art, or equivalent)
  • Web Gallery of Art
  • Internet Archive
  • Library of Congress
  • Yorck Project scans and similar pre-1929 photographic reproductions of older works

Each image carries (or will carry) a small attribution noting the holding institution or Commons file and its public-domain status. If a portrait has not yet been sourced, a tradition-colored monogram is shown instead of a photograph.

Texts — Reproduction Rules

Full text is reproduced on-site only when the work is genuinely in the public domain. Good source libraries by tradition include:

Catholic — New Advent (Catholic Encyclopedia, Summa, Fathers), Catholic Library, Catena Aurea (verified per entry), Vatican.va (most post-1929 material is copyrighted; we link out).

Orthodox — CCEL.org, OrthodoxWiki (reference), Holy Trinity Orthodox Mission / Orthodox Christian Information Center (license checked per document), Internet Archive pre-1923 editions.

Protestant — CCEL.org (Calvin, Luther, Puritans), Monergism (rights verified per item), Project Gutenberg, older commentaries on BibleHub / Blue Letter Bible.

For any work that is not public domain (most 20th- and 21st-century theology, most post-1929 magisterial documents), the site shows an original summary plus an outbound “Read at [Source]” link with attribution. We never reproduce copyrighted text.

Bible Translations

The Sacred Texts library aims to include multiple public-domain translations suitable across the three traditions:

  • King James Version (1611)
  • Douay-Rheims
  • American Standard Version (1901)
  • Brenton’s Septuagint (for an Orthodox-friendly Old Testament)

All of the above are public domain. Chapter-by-chapter readers will expand as content is added.

Tradition Color Coding

Early Church (pre-1054) — bronze Catholic — gold Eastern Orthodox — purple Protestant — blue

Figures active before the Great Schism of 1054 receive the early-church bronze ring, since the later categories do not yet apply. After 1054 each figure is coded according to the tradition that formed them.

Contact & Corrections

This is an expanding archive. If you notice a factual error, a missing attribution, or a portrait that can be replaced with a better-documented public-domain image, the content will be corrected. The goal is accuracy and reverence, not speed.
